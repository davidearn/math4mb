\newcommand{\SIRintro}{%
  Consider the standard SIR model with vital dynamics,
  \begin{subequations}\label{E:SIR}
    \begin{align}
      \frac{dS}{dt} &= \mu N -\frac{\beta}{N} SI - \mu S\\
      \noalign{\vspace{8pt}}
      \frac{dI}{dt} &= \frac{\beta}{N} SI - \gamma I - \mu I\\
      \noalign{\vspace{8pt}}
      \frac{dR}{dt} &= \gamma I - \mu R
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  where $S$, $I$ and $R$ denote the numbers of susceptible, infectious
  and removed individuals, respectively, and $N=S+I+R>0$ is the total
  population size. The \emph{per capita} rates of birth and death are
  the same (both are equal to $\mu>0$). As usual, $\beta>0$ is the
  transmission rate and $\gamma>0$ is the recovery rate.
}

\long\def\NotesOnLaSalle{
\section*{Notes on LaSalle's invariance principle}\label{S:LaSalleNotes}

The Lyapunov theorems stated in Assignment 1 distinguish stability
from attraction. A positive definite $L$ with $\dot L\le0$ proves
stability, but those theorems require $\dot L<0$ away from the
equilibrium (or invariant set) to prove attraction. When $\dot L=0$
at other points, the following result can establish attraction by
examining which solutions can remain in that zero-derivative set.

\setcounter{theorem}{2}
\begin{theorem}[LaSalle's invariance principle]\label{Th:LaSalle}
Consider $X'=F(X)$, where $F$ is continuously differentiable on an
open set $D\subset\reals^n$. Let $K\subset D$ be compact (closed and
bounded) and forward-invariant, and let $L:D\to\reals$ be continuously
differentiable with
\[
 \dot L(X)=\nabla L(X)\cdot F(X)\le0 \quad\text{for all }X\in K.
\]
Define $E=\{X\in K:\dot L(X)=0\}$, and let $M$ be the largest
invariant subset of $E$. Then the distance from every solution starting
in $K$ to $M$ tends to zero as time tends to infinity. In particular,
if $M$ consists of a single equilibrium, every such solution converges
to that equilibrium.
\end{theorem}

Here the largest invariant subset means all the complete trajectories
that remain in $E$ for all positive and negative times. The set $E$
itself need not be invariant: a solution may have $\dot L=0$ at one
instant but then leave $E$, after which $L$ decreases again. LaSalle's
principle rules out long-term behaviour outside $M$. It does not say
that every point where $\dot L=0$ is an equilibrium.

For LaSalle's treatment, see Chapter~2, \S6, pp.\,29--32 of
\cite{LaSalle1976}. Accessible textbook expositions are given by
Wiggins~\cite[Chapter~7, Theorem~2]{Wiggins2017} and
Teschl~\cite[\S6.6, Theorem~6.14, p.\,202]{Teschl2012}.
}
